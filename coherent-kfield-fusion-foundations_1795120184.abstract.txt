ABSTRACT_BEGIN
A low energy fusion pathway must coordinate access to a reacting state, the source of control work, nuclear product formation, and restoration of its mediator. This paper assimilates eighteen core K-Field papers within a twenty-five-artifact source inventory, including the current continuum, capture, antipodal-network, curvature, Mass Bridge, interference, and multiport results together with earlier rotating-mass and photon-energy coupling work. It derives a composite candidate in which an advancing Earth-referenced field phase supplies reciprocal work, differential and pair-compatible couplings register the incoming state, coordinated controls reduce internal state deformation, and a product-selective doorway completes the reaction. A fixed-phase two-state compensation identity is extended to complex controls and to a multichannel projection criterion. Fresh diagonalization of the archived neutron–proton E1 product matrix gives 93.36469 microbarn for its optimal eight-mode coherent state, compared with 19.13294 microbarn for the equal-flux incoherent mixture, a factor of 4.87979 in that partial channel at 100 keV. Explicit position averaging quantifies the dependence on local phase registration. A separate dimensionless model integrates ordered transfer, product release, mediator restoration, and phase-receiver work in one cycle: the stipulated product probability is 0.9999679, with conservation residuals below 8 × 10⁻¹⁵ in model units. The resulting pathway is established as a sufficient, internally consistent reduced mechanism under stated coupling assumptions. Its realization as low energy charged-particle fusion remains determined by the common microscopic receiving operator, charged entrance continuum, preparation cost, and product spectrum. The paper formulates those remaining quantities as a finite identification problem and provides reproducible calculations, predictions, source locators, and an audit correction record.
